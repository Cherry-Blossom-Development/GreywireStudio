# Flashover breadboard, stage 2: the Pico, endless knobs and digital pots

Nov 5, 2026

Stage 2 swaps the three ordinary pots on the stage 1 breadboard for the real thing:
- **Three endless knobs (encoders).** They are read by a Raspberry Pi Pico, which runs the Flashover firmware (`../firmware/`).
- **The three digital pots.** The firmware sets them. They sit on a small breakout board that JLCPCB makes and assembles.

The sound stays the same. What changes is that the knobs now work as they will on the finished pedal: they turn endlessly, they speed up when spun, and the pedal remembers the settings after power-off.

Background: `FLASHOVER_BREADBOARD_OPTIONS.md` (Option B, stages 2–5). The stage 1 build is `FLASHOVER_BREADBOARD_STAGE1_HOLES.md`; finish it first, since stage 2 builds on it.

The breakout board carries the **LED driver too**, so one board order covers stages 2 and 3. In stage 2 the LED driver is only powered and connected, so the Pico can check that it answers.

## The breakout board

KiCad project: `Flashover_Breakout/`, generated by `scripts/gen_breakout.py` (schematic) and `scripts/gen_breakout_pcb.py` (board).
- **Size:** 77.7 × 29.8 mm, two layers.
- **What's on it:** the same chips, I2C addresses, supporting parts and part numbers as the main board, so the firmware runs unchanged:
  - U7 Gain pot (100k)
  - U8 Tone and U9 Level pots (10k)
  - U10 LED driver, with its current resistor R33 and the I2C pull-ups
- **Two differences from the main board:**
  - The LED driver is the QFN-44 package, which JLCPCB stocks. The main board uses the TQFP-48, which is the same chip.
  - Each pot has 100 nF decoupling on both supplies (C101–C106). The main board is missing these (see "Open points").

**Pins:** two rows of 30, 17.78 mm (0.7") apart, like a Pico, so the board straddles a breadboard's centre groove. Row 1 goes in row **c** of the breadboard and row 2 in row **h**, leaving holes a–b and i–j free beside every pin. Every pin is named on the board.

| Pin | Row 1 (top) | Row 2 (bottom) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 9V (the pots' audio supply) | 3V3 (logic supply, from the Pico) |
| 2 | GND | GND |
| 3 | GA: Gain end A | SDA (pots' I2C bus) |
| 4 | GW: Gain wiper | SCL |
| 5 | GB: Gain end B | GND |
| 6 | TA: Tone end A | EN (LED driver on/off) |
| 7 | TW: Tone wiper | LSDA (LED driver's I2C bus) |
| 8 | TB: Tone end B | LSCL |
| 9 | LA: Level end A | GND |
| 10 | LW: Level wiper | GND |
| 11 | LB: Level end B | GND |
| 12 | GND | 5V (LED driver supply) |
| 13–30 | LED outputs 18, 17 … 1 (counting down) | LED outputs 19, 20 … 36 (counting up) |

The LED outputs count round the board the way pins count round a chip: 1 at the right end of row 1, along to 18 at its left, then 19 at the left end of row 2, along to 36 at its right. That order lets every track run straight out from the LED driver without crossing; the numbers on the board say which is which. Outputs 1–11 are the Gain ring, 12–22 Tone, 23–33 Level, 34 Soft, 35 Hard and 36 On.

### Ordering it from JLCPCB

The files are in `Flashover_Breakout/fab/`:
- `Flashover_Breakout_gerbers.zip`: the board itself
- `Flashover_Breakout_bom.csv`: the parts, with LCSC part numbers
- `Flashover_Breakout_cpl.csv`: where each part goes

1. At jlcpcb.com, choose **Order now** and upload the gerbers zip. Keep the defaults: 2 layers, 1.6 mm, any colour. 5 boards is the minimum.
2. Turn on **PCB Assembly**: top side, quantity **2**. Choose Standard assembly if the site asks, because the LED driver's pins are 0.4 mm apart.
3. Upload the BOM and CPL files. JLCPCB matches each line to a part.
   - The LED driver and all the small parts were in stock in November 2026.
   - **The digital pots may not be.** LCSC showed the 10k pot nearly gone and the 100k at zero. If a pot line shows as out of stock, choose JLCPCB's **Global Sourcing** for it: they buy it from DigiKey or Mouser, which both had stock. That adds about a week.
4. On the placement preview, check that each chip's pin 1 dot sits on the board's pin 1 mark. It's usual for JLCPCB to need a rotation corrected here, and their engineers also check it before building.
5. The two 30-pin header rows are **not** assembled; you solder them yourself (60 easy through-hole joints). Push the headers into a breadboard first, lay the board on top and solder, so the pins come out straight.

Expected cost: about $40–70 for 2 assembled boards, depending on shipping and Global Sourcing. Two boards, so there's a spare.

## How it's laid out

- **Breadboard 2:**
  - The Pico at the left, straddling the groove, with its USB socket facing left.
  - The breakout board to its right, straddling the groove with row 1 in row c. Its pins 1 are on the left.
  - 30 + 20 columns, with a gap between, fit in the board's 63.
- **Breadboard 3:**
  - The L7805 and its capacitors.
  - The encoder filter parts (one 10k and one 10 nF per encoder signal).
  - The buttons, and the encoder wires.
- **Between the boards:**
  - The 9 V and GND rails join breadboard 1 (the audio).
  - The digital pots' 9 wires go back to where the stage 1 pots plugged in.

A hole-by-hole guide, like stage 1's, comes once the parts are here, so it can match your real boards.

## Connections

**Pico pins** are given by GPIO name and the Pico's own pin number (1–40, printed on its underside; pin 1 is next to the USB socket).

### Power

| Net | Connects |
| --- | --- |
| +9V | Breadboard 1 top + rail, L7805 input (pin 1), 330 nF, breakout row 1 pin 1 |
| +5V | L7805 output (pin 3), 100 nF, 1N5817 anode (plain end), breakout row 2 pin 12 |
| VSYS | 1N5817 cathode (striped end), Pico VSYS (pin 39) |
| +3V3 | Pico 3V3(OUT) (pin 36), breakout row 2 pin 1, the 10k pull-ups |
| GND | Breadboard 1 − rail, L7805 middle pin (2), both capacitors, Pico GND (pin 38 and any other GND pin), breakout row 1 pins 2 and 12, row 2 pins 2, 5 and 9–11 |

The Pico's 3.3 V regulator feeds the pots' logic, the pull-ups and the LED driver's bus, as U6 does on the main board. The 1N5817 stops the regulator and a USB cable fighting when both are connected.

### Digital pots to the audio circuit

These replace the stage 1 pot wires. Unplug each pot's wires and put the breakout's wire in the same hole.

| Breakout pin | Was (stage 1) | Hole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Row 1 pin 3, GA | Gain lug 3 | a6 |
| Row 1 pin 4, GW **and** pin 5, GB | Gain lugs 2 and 1 (joined) | a4 |
| Row 1 pin 6, TA | Tone lug 1 | j24 |
| Row 1 pin 7, TW | Tone lug 2 | j40 |
| Row 1 pin 8, TB | Tone lug 3 | j21 |
| Row 1 pin 9, LA | Level lug 3 | j35 |
| Row 1 pin 10, LW | Level lug 2 | j48 |
| Row 1 pin 11, LB | Level lug 1 | bottom + rail (VB) |

GW and GB both go to a4, because on the board the Gain pot is wired as a variable resistor (wiper joined to one end), as the stage 1 pot was.

### Pico to the breakout board

| Pico | Breakout | Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GP4 (pin 6) | Row 2 pin 3, SDA | Pots' I2C data |
| GP5 (pin 7) | Row 2 pin 4, SCL | Pots' I2C clock |
| GP17 (pin 22) | Row 2 pin 6, EN | LED driver on |
| GP18 (pin 24) | Row 2 pin 7, LSDA | LED driver's I2C data |
| GP19 (pin 25) | Row 2 pin 8, LSCL | LED driver's I2C clock |

The pull-up resistors for both buses are on the breakout board.

### Encoders and buttons

Each encoder signal gets a 10k pull-up to +3V3. A and B also get a 10 nF capacitor to GND, which smooths contact bounce as on the control board. Each encoder's C pin and second switch pin go to GND.

| Encoder pin | Gain (SW1) | Tone (SW2) | Level (SW3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | GP6 (pin 9) | GP9 (pin 12) | GP12 (pin 16) |
| B | GP7 (pin 10) | GP10 (pin 14) | GP13 (pin 17) |
| Switch | GP8 (pin 11) | GP11 (pin 15) | GP14 (pin 19) |

| Button | Pico | Parts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Footswitch stand-in (SW5) | GP15 (pin 20) | 10k pull-up to +3V3, 100 nF to GND (C42), other side of the button to GND |
| Soft/Hard (SW4), stage 3 | GP16 (pin 21) | 10k pull-up to +3V3 (R32), other side to GND. It can wait until stage 3 |

**The 3PDT footswitch stays** as stage 1's true bypass. The new button stands in for the finished pedal's soft-touch footswitch: it toggles the firmware's on/off setting and is used for setup mode. Until the bypass relay arrives in stage 5, the 3PDT is what you hear switch.

**The Soft/Hard toggle stays** too, until stage 5. Leave the firmware in Soft mode (don't wire or press SW4 yet). In Hard mode the firmware cuts Level to 38% to match loudness, which only makes sense once the relay switches the diodes.

## Loading the firmware and testing

Build output is in `../firmware/build/`:
- **`flashover_debug_full.uf2`:** the loader plus the firmware with a report on the Pico's USB serial port. Use this one for stage 2.
- **`flashover_full.uf2`:** the same without the report.
- `flashover_debug.uf2` and `flashover.uf2` are the firmware alone, for later updates once the loader is on.

1. Hold the Pico's BOOTSEL button while plugging its USB cable into the computer. It appears as a drive; copy `flashover_debug_full.uf2` onto it. The Pico restarts by itself.
2. Open the Pico's serial port (any serial terminal at any speed, for example PuTTY, or the Arduino IDE's Serial Monitor). Within 2 seconds of power-up it prints:
   ```
   Flashover 0.1.0 debug
     pot U7 Gain  answered
     pot U8 Tone  answered
     pot U9 Level answered
     LED driver answered
   ```
   **NOT ANSWERING** means that chip didn't reply. Check its wiring, the 9 V and 3.3 V supplies, and SDA/SCL. The LED driver also needs the 5 V and EN wires.
3. Turn each knob. A line appears for every change, for example:
   ```
   gain  70  tone  64  level  64  Soft  off  | pots 141 126 129 | ch -  pos 0
   ```
   - The first three numbers are the settings, 0–127. "pots" shows the codes sent to the chips, 0–255.
   - **Check 1:** turning a knob clockwise should raise its number. If one goes the wrong way, it's a one-word change in `../firmware/pico/board.h` (`ENC_REVERSE`), and a rebuild.
   - **Check 2:** each knob changes the sound as the old pot did. Gain up is more distortion, Tone up is brighter, Level up is louder.
   - **Check 3:** a slow turn moves 1 per click; a quick spin moves faster.
4. **Memory:** set the knobs, wait 3 seconds, unplug everything and plug back in. The report should show the same settings.
5. **Setup mode:**
   1. Unplug the 9 V supply and the USB cable.
   2. Hold the footswitch button while plugging the USB cable back in, then plug in the 9 V. The Pico has to start up with the button held, and with USB connected so the report can be seen.
   3. The report says "setup mode". Turn Gain to pick a channel, and press the button to save.

**Hum while the USB cable is in:** the computer's ground joins the amp's through the cable, which can hum. It doesn't hurt anything. For listening tests, unplug the USB cable and run from the 9 V supply alone; the settings and the firmware stay.

## Open points

- **Main board: no decoupling on the digital pots.** The datasheet calls for a capacitor on each pot's VL and V+ pins, and the main board has none. The breakout has them (C101–C106). The main board needs 6 small capacitors added before it is ordered; that means adding them to the hand-routed layout.
- **Main board LED driver package.** The main board uses the TQFP-48 version (IS31FL3236A-TQLS4). In November 2026 JLCPCB stocked only the QFN-44 version (IS31FL3236A-QFLS2), the one on the breakout. Before ordering the main board, either source the TQFP for JLCPCB or switch the control board to the QFN.
- **Encoder direction and knob feel:** check on the breadboard (see testing).

## Glossary

- **Breakout board:** a small board that carries a tiny chip and brings its pins out to a size you can plug into a breadboard.
- **Encoder:** an endless knob that gives two on/off signals as it turns; their order gives the direction.
- **I2C:** a two-wire bus (data and clock) the Pico uses to talk to the pots and the LED driver. Each chip has its own address.
- **Pull-up resistor:** holds a wire at 3.3 V until a switch or chip pulls it to ground.
- **Decoupling capacitor:** a small capacitor right at a chip's supply pin, which steadies the supply when the chip switches.
- **Gerbers, BOM, CPL:** the three files a board maker needs: the board's layers, the parts list, and each part's position and rotation.
- **QFN / TQFP:** chip packages. A QFN has its contacts underneath, at the edges; a TQFP has small legs. Both need machine assembly at this pin spacing.
